Almost every complaint about copying on a Mac traces back to the same design decision: the system clipboard
holds exactly one item and discards it the instant you copy the next one. What follows is the list of problems
that creates, and what CopyPaste Pro does about each. Version 3.8.4 is the current build, priced at $18.99.

## Why It Happens

None of these are bugs in macOS; they are consequences of a single-slot clipboard that was designed when
copying meant moving one paragraph at a time. Modern work does not look like that. A normal hour involves a
tracking number, two links, a code snippet, someone's address and a chunk of text you are reformatting, and
the system model quietly discards five of those six.

CopyPaste Pro's approach is to stop treating a copy as disposable. It operates invisibly in the background,
remembering what you take, and splits the results into two layers: a rolling Clip History for the recent past
and a permanent Clip Archive for things you return to constantly. That split is the important idea. A
clipboard manager mac utility that only kept a history would still lose your signature block eventually; one
that only kept an archive would need you to decide, in advance, what mattered.

## Prevention Tips

- Archive anything you have copied three times. If it happened three times it will happen thirty.
- Get into the habit of opening the Clip Editor before pasting into anything formatted — it costs two seconds
  and prevents most cleanup work in the destination app.
- Learn one hotkey properly rather than five badly. Keyboard access to a specific clipboard is where the real
  speed lives once copypaste pro download mac is running.
- Use drag and drop between the clip browser and your applications instead of round-tripping through the
  system clipboard.
- Let the contextual menus do the work when your hands are already on the mouse; there is no prize for
  keyboard purity.
- Treat clipboard history mac storage as memory-bound rather than count-bound — the number of clipboards is
  unlimited depending on the memory available, so a very long history on a small machine is worth pruning.
